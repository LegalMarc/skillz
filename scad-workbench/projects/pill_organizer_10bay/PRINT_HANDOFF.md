## Task

Slice and print the pill organizer **in two stages with a hard stop between
them**. Work on Marc's computer via `device_bash`. The slicer is **Elegoo
Slicer**. Do not rebuild anything in the container.

**Files:** `LegalMarc/skillz`, `origin/main`, under
`scad-workbench/projects/pill_organizer_10bay/build/`. Pull a fresh clone or
`git pull` an existing one onto his machine — do not work from an older copy.
The design went through seven revisions and two independent review rounds, and
several of the things that were wrong early look fine in a render.

## The thing to understand before you start

Two test parts share the stage-1 plate and **they prove different things. Do
not conflate them.**

- **The calibration coupon is full scale and is the only thing on that plate
  that tests fit.** It carries a ladder of five male rail stubs and five snap
  barbs at −0.15 / −0.075 / 0 / +0.075 / +0.15 mm off nominal, plus a loose
  groove block and a mouth block to mate them with. Eight islands, no supports.
- **The maquette is 0.42 scale and tests form and printability only.**
  Clearances do not scale. The 0.35 mm rail clearance becomes 0.147 mm and the
  1.2 mm snap tab becomes 0.5 mm. **The maquette lids will not clip properly
  and that is expected, not a defect.** Reporting "the small lids don't snap"
  as a problem means the plate has been misread.

What the maquette *does* answer is the pair of open questions the geometry
cannot settle on its own: whether the 44.8° vaulted chute ceiling comes out
clean, and whether the 20.3 mm porch bridge over the splitter rib spans without
drooping.

Open `build/maquette/test_print_plate_256.3mf`. Core-spec 3MF, four named

|---|---|---|
| `pill_organizer_body_x0.42` | 98.7 × 71.7 × 59.2 | (15, 15) |
| `pill_organizer_pick_lid_x0.42` | 96.2 × 39.9 × 7.2 | (130, 15) |
| `pill_organizer_fill_lid_x0.42` | 94.0 × 47.4 × 6.3 | (130, 70) |
| `calibration_coupon` | 170.0 × 70.8 × 22.0 | (15, 135) |

If anything differs, stop and say so rather than adjusting it. The plate is
generated by `build/maquette/make_plate.py`, which asserts a 10 mm plate margin
and a 10 mm gap between every pair; regenerate with it after any re-export,
never by dragging parts in the slicer.

### Non-negotiable slicer settings

- **Supports OFF.** Every part is designed support-free. The crossing chute is
  an *enclosed* passage 105 mm long and the accessory cubby is a blind pocket —
  support material generated in either cannot be removed and ruins the part.
  The slicer will flag overhangs anyway: the 44.8° vaulted chute ceiling, the
  rib-carried porch ceiling, the lid seat ledges. Those are intended and inside
  the printable band.
- **Do not re-orient, and do not let auto-arrange rotate anything.** Every
  object is already in its correct print orientation sitting on z = 0.
  Revision 5 shipped a pick lid standing on its skirt with the plate 14 mm in
  the air; that is fixed, and it must stay fixed.
- **Use the printer and filament profile already configured** in his Elegoo
  Slicer. Do not invent or import one. PLA or PETG. The 3MF's own description
  metadata suggests 0.2 mm layers and 3 perimeters.
- Brim is fine, and sensible for the coupon's small islands.

Try the Elegoo Slicer CLI first — run `--help` and find its actual flags rather
than assuming Orca's, even though Elegoo Slicer derives from it. Fall back to
asking Marc to drive the GUI if there is no usable CLI.

### Report, then STOP

Report estimated time and filament for the plate, any overhang or thin-wall
warning with the object and location it names, and a preview or screenshot of
the sliced plate. **Then stop. Do not start stage 2.**

## The gate: reading the coupon

After the print, the coupon result is what actually decides whether stage 2 can
proceed.

- **Rail.** Drop the loose groove block over each of the five stubs. The one
  that seats with hand pressure and does not rock is the clearance.
- **Snap.** Press each mini lid into the mouth block and lift it out by its
  lip. It should click both ways and hold through a shake.

**Report which column won for each.** If either is anything other than the
middle (0.0) column, the matching value in `params.scad` needs updating and the
full-size parts re-exporting from `print_export.scad` **before** the body is
printed:

| Fit | Parameter | Current |
|---|---|---|
| Rail in its groove | `rail_clear` (per flank) | 0.35 |
| Snap barb in its pocket | `fill_tab_barb` | 1.1 |
| | `fill_lid_clear` | 0.30 |

Real barb engagement is `fill_tab_barb − fill_lid_clear` = 0.8, so the
clearance matters as much as the barb.

This is the whole reason the coupon exists. The body is a ~1,138 cm³ print and
discovering a bad rail fit afterwards is the expensive failure. `doctor.py`
reports no calibration profile for this machine, which is why every fit in the
design is geometry-only (tier 2) until this coupon says otherwise.

## Stage 2 — full size, only after Marc confirms

**Three separate plates. They cannot share one** — each part is nearly the full
bed width.

| Part | Size (mm) | Solid volume |
|---|---|---|
| `build/print_ready/body.stl` | 235 × 170.8 × 141 | 1,138 cm³ |
| `build/print_ready/pick_lid.stl` | 229 × 95.1 × 17.1 | 67 cm³ |
| `build/print_ready/fill_lid.stl` | 223.8 × 112.9 × 15.0 | 71 cm³ |

Same rules: supports off, no re-orientation, configured profile. The design
targets a 256 × 256 × 256 bed with 243 mm usable after skirt and exclusion
zones, so check the 235 mm body still clears whatever brim is set.

The body is the long one. Print it last, after the lids have confirmed the
fits on a part that costs an hour rather than a day.

## If something goes wrong

Say exactly what failed and stop. Do not improvise around it, do not edit the
STLs or the 3MF by hand, and do not re-orient a part to make a warning go away
— the orientations are load-bearing and the reasons are in `README.md` under
Bill of materials.
